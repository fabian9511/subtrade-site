"Free" is the most expensive word in construction software, because the price just moves somewhere you're not looking. There's nothing wrong with free tools — a spreadsheet is genuinely the right answer for some things, and we'll say where. But if you're a subcontractor searching for free subcontractor management software to run the whole business on, it's worth doing the real math first, because the bill still arrives. It just arrives as rekeyed hours, extras nobody signed, and invoices that went out a week late.

We ran Quality Gypsum Services, a commercial drywall contractor in Calgary, Alberta, on spreadsheets and free apps for years before we built SubTrade. This is the honest accounting of what that cost — and how to tell when free is fine and when it's quietly bleeding you.

## What "free" usually means

The free options subs actually reach for fall into three buckets, and they fail in different ways.

### Spreadsheets

Free, universal, and genuinely good at a few things. The trouble starts when the same numbers have to live in five sheets at once — a timesheet, a job-cost tab, an invoice, a change-order log, and a summary — and every one of them is maintained by hand. The spreadsheet isn't wrong; it's just that keeping five copies of the truth in sync is a part-time job nobody's being paid for.

### Free tiers of paid platforms

Most "free plan" construction tools are a doorway. They cap you at one or two projects, a couple of users, or the features that matter, and the moment you're actually running the business on it you've hit the wall and the per-seat pricing kicks in — usually priced for an office, not a field crew.

### Generic free apps stitched together

A free scheduling app, a free photo app, a free forms app, a group chat. Each is fine alone. Together they're four places to look, none of which talk to each other, and the job-cost picture lives in none of them.

## The real cost of free, in numbers

Free tools don't send an invoice, so the cost hides. Here's where it actually lands, using round numbers a mid-sized sub would recognize.

| Hidden cost | Where it shows up | Rough monthly bill |
|---|---|---|
| Rekeying field data | Office admin retyping timesheets into job cost and invoices | 6–10 hours |
| Late invoicing | Progress billing that slips a week because the backup isn't ready | Cash-flow drag on every draw |
| Unsigned extras | Change orders lost because there's no paper trail | Often more than a year of software fees, on one job |
| Version confusion | "Which spreadsheet is the current one?" | Hours, plus the occasional wrong bid |
| No live job cost | Finding out a job lost money weeks after you could act | The expensive one |

That last row is the whole game. The single most valuable thing software does for a sub is tell you, by Thursday afternoon, whether this week's job is making money — while you can still change crew size, chase an extra, or fix the sequence. A free stack almost never does that, because the labour data is sitting untyped in a foreman's pocket. Everything else is a rounding error next to a job you couldn't see going bad.

## Where free genuinely wins

This isn't a pitch to buy software for everything. A spreadsheet is the right tool when:

- You're **modelling a one-off** — a quick what-if on a bid, a scratch calc you'll throw away.
- You're a **one- or two-person shop** on a single job at a time, where the "five copies of the truth" problem doesn't exist yet because there's only one job.
- You need a **calculator, not a system.** For statutory holdback and US retainage, you don't need a subscription at all — our [holdback calculator](/construction-holdback-calculator/) and [retainage calculator](/construction-retainage-calculator/) are free, need no signup, and export a one-page PDF. Use them and keep your money.

The line is roughly this: free is fine while the data only lives in one place and one head. The moment the same numbers have to be true in a timesheet, a job cost, and an invoice at the same time, free stops being free.

## How to know you've outgrown free

You've crossed the line when any of these are true:

1. Someone spends **half a day a week retyping** the same numbers between sheets.
2. You **found out a job lost money after it closed**, not while it was running.
3. You've **eaten an extra** in the last year because you couldn't prove who directed it.
4. Two people have argued about **which spreadsheet is current.**
5. Your **field crew has grown** past what one person can track in their head.

Any two of those and the "free" stack is costing you more than a subscription would. ## FAQ

**Is there genuinely free subcontractor management software?**

There are free spreadsheets, free tiers of paid platforms, and free single-purpose apps you can stitch together. All of them are genuinely free to start; the cost shows up later as rekeyed data, late invoices, and lost extras rather than as a monthly bill.

**When is a spreadsheet the right choice?**

When the data only has to live in one place — a one-off bid model, a scratch calculation, or a one-person shop on a single job. Spreadsheets break down once the same numbers have to be true in a timesheet, a job cost, and an invoice at the same time.

**What does free software actually cost a subcontractor?**

Mostly hidden labour and missed money: hours spent retyping field data, invoices that slip because the backup isn't ready, and change orders lost for want of a paper trail. On a single job, one unsigned extra can cost more than a year of software fees.

**How do I know I've outgrown free tools?**

Common signs: someone spends half a day a week retyping numbers, you learned a job lost money only after it closed, you ate an extra you couldn't prove, or people argue about which spreadsheet is current. Any two of those and free is costing more than a subscription.
