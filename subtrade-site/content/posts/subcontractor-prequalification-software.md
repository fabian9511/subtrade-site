Most subcontractors think they lose work at the bid. A lot of them actually lose it before that — at prequalification, the stage where a general contractor decides who even gets invited to quote. If your paperwork isn't current when the invitation list goes out, you're not in the running, and nobody sends you a rejection to tell you so.

We learned this the hard way at Quality Gypsum Services, the commercial drywall contractor in Calgary, Alberta where SubTrade was built. Twice in one year we missed shortlists because a WCB clearance letter had lapsed by a week, or an insurance certificate named the wrong holder. The estimate would have been fine. We never got to write it.

This is a rundown of what prequalification is, what a GC actually checks, and how subcontractor prequalification software keeps your package ready so a request never catches you flat.

## What subcontractor prequalification is

Prequalification is a general contractor's vetting process. Before a project goes out to bid — and often before a firm even joins an owner's approved-vendor list — the GC gathers evidence that each sub can carry the work without becoming a risk. Financial stability, safety record, insurance, bonding capacity, licensing, and a track record on similar jobs. The bigger the project and the more safety-sensitive the scope, the harder the screen.

On large or public work you'll often meet a third-party system rather than a paper form — ISNetworld, Avetta, ComplyWorks, or a GC's own portal. The categories are the same underneath. Someone on your team fills in a questionnaire, uploads documents, and keeps them from going stale.

## What a general contractor checks

The exact form varies, but nearly every prequalification asks for the same six things. Knowing where each one lives — and who owns keeping it current — is half the battle.

| Category | What they want | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| Insurance | Certificate of insurance with correct limits and the GC named as additional insured | Your broker |
| Workers' comp | A valid WCB clearance letter (in Alberta) showing your account is in good standing | Provincial board |
| Safety | COR or SECOR certificate, your safety manual, and lost-time and recordable injury rates | Your safety program |
| Financials | Bonding capacity, a bank reference, sometimes reviewed statements | Your accountant or surety |
| Experience | Three to five comparable projects with values, dates and references | Your own project history |
| Capacity | Current backlog, crew size, and equipment relevant to the scope | Your operations |

Two of these — WCB clearance and insurance certificates — expire on a schedule and are the single most common reason a package gets kicked back. A clearance letter is only current for a set window, and a certificate names a specific holder for a specific project. A generic copy from last spring rarely satisfies a careful GC.

## Why subs get filtered out early

### The paperwork is scattered

The insurance certificate is an email from your broker. The WCB letter is a login you use twice a year. Your injury rates are in a binder, or in someone's head. When a prequal lands with a Friday deadline, assembling all of it is a scramble — and a scramble is where the wrong-dated document sneaks through.

### Nobody owns the expiry dates

A certificate that lapses doesn't announce itself. It just quietly stops being acceptable, and you find out when a shortlist skips you. The fix is boring and it works: one place that tracks every document and its expiry, so renewals happen before anyone asks.

### Safety stats aren't at your fingertips

More GCs now gate prequalification on hard numbers — total recordable incident rate, lost-time claims, days since last incident. If those come out of a shoebox of paper forms, you can't answer quickly or defend the figure. Logging safety activity as it happens turns that question into a lookup instead of an audit. A system that captures [safety and custom forms](/construction-management-features/safety-custom-forms/) from the field — toolbox talks, inspections, incident reports — means the number is already there when a prequal asks for it.

### Experience is real but undocumented

You've done forty jobs like the one on offer. Proving it with values, dates, contacts and photos is another matter if that history lives across old emails and a folder of invoices. A running record of completed projects turns "trust us" into a reference sheet you can attach in minutes.

## How software keeps a package current

Subcontractor prequalification software isn't a magic form-filler. What it does is hold the underlying evidence in one current place so that responding to any prequal — paper form, ISNetworld, or a GC's portal — is assembly rather than archaeology.

The parts that matter:

**A live document register** with every certificate, licence and clearance letter, each tagged with its expiry, so renewals are scheduled instead of discovered late.

**Safety records that build themselves.** When your foremen log toolbox talks, inspections and incidents from the field, your recordable and lost-time rates are a report, not a reconstruction.

**A project history you can cite.** Completed jobs with values, dates, the GC's name and a few photos — the exact backup an experience section wants.

**Labour and cost history** that stands behind your capacity claims. When a GC asks whether you can carry another two million in backlog, real [time tracking](/time-tracking/) and job-cost data answers honestly rather than optimistically.

None of this is exotic. It's the same operational data a well-run sub already captures to run its jobs. Prequalification just asks you to hand a slice of it over on demand. ## If you prequalify your own second-tier subs

Larger subs sublet — a drywall contractor hiring a painter, a mechanical firm hiring insulation. The moment you do, you're the one running the prequalification, and their lapsed certificate becomes your liability on site. The same register that keeps your documents current can hold theirs: collect the certificate, name yourself as additional insured, confirm the clearance letter, and set the expiry reminder. It's the flip side of the coin, and the discipline is identical.

## What to look for

You don't need a dedicated prequalification product on day one. You need your operational data organized well enough that a request is a five-minute export. Before you buy anything specialized, ask whether the system you already run your jobs in can produce a current certificate register, live safety stats, and a documented project list. If it can, you're most of the way there.

## FAQ

**What is subcontractor prequalification software?**

It's a tool that keeps the evidence a general contractor asks for at prequalification — insurance certificates, WCB clearance, safety records, project history and capacity data — current and in one place, so you can respond to any prequal request quickly and accurately.

**What do general contractors check when prequalifying a subcontractor?**

Typically insurance and additional-insured status, valid workers' compensation coverage, safety certification and injury rates, financial and bonding capacity, comparable project experience, and current backlog and crew capacity.

**Why do subcontractors fail prequalification?**

Most often because a document has expired — a lapsed WCB clearance letter or an insurance certificate naming the wrong holder — or because safety stats and project references can't be produced before the deadline. The work itself is rarely the problem.

**Do I need special software, or will my existing system work?**

If your current system tracks certificates with expiry dates, captures safety forms from the field, and holds a documented project and cost history, you can assemble most prequals from it. A dedicated product mainly helps if you respond to prequalification questionnaires constantly.

**How is prequalification different from bidding?**

Prequalification decides whether you're allowed to bid; bidding is pricing the work once you're invited. You can be the sharpest estimator in town and still never get the chance if your prequal package isn't current.

**Does prequalification apply to the subs I hire too?**

Yes. If you sublet any scope, you should prequalify those firms the same way a GC prequalifies you — collect their certificates, name yourself as additional insured, and track the expiry dates, because their lapse becomes your exposure on site.
